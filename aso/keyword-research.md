# Keyword Research — Peptide Tracker · Stackr

Built across the app's five jobs (tracker, logger, reminder, inventory, reconstitution/syringe calculator) and
its three distinct audiences, each of which searches with different vocabulary:

- **GLP-1 / weight-loss users** — the biggest, fastest-growing volume driver
- **TRT / hormone / PED users** — steady, durable demand
- **Peptide biohackers** — rising; rely heavily on web dosage calculators

## Market demand context (mid-2026)

- **GLP-1 is the dominant volume driver and still accelerating.** US users ~10M (2025) → projected ~25M by
  2030; prescriptions up 587% (2019–2024); oral semaglutide launched early 2026. Mainstream non-diabetic users
  are flooding in.
- **Tirzepatide is rising faster than semaglutide.** **Retatrutide** (triple agonist / "GLP-3") is the
  fastest-growing *emerging* term and is still under-served by native apps — the single best early-mover bet.
- **Peptides/biohacking** (BPC-157, TB-500, ipamorelin, CJC-1295) is a clear secondary growth wave, heavily
  reliant on web calculators — apps merely *list* compounds rather than rank on the exact terms.
- **TRT** is steady and durable, with a less-funded competitive field than GLP-1.

## Key research notes

- **Include both hyphenated and unhyphenated forms** (`bpc 157` vs `bpc-157`, `glp1` vs `glp-1`) — App Store
  search treats them differently and users type both. Apple's stemming is weak; don't assume one form covers all.
- **Calculator-only positioning is weak** — free web tools (peptidecalc.io, peppal, peptidefox, glp3planner)
  satisfy calculator intent without a download. Position as a *tracker that includes* a great calculator.
- **The money phrase nobody owns cleanly:** `peptide reconstitution calculator` / `reconstitution calculator`
  is only ever assembled passively via cross-field combination. Capturing it as a contiguous phrase in the
  subtitle is a real opportunity (see `metadata-recommendations.md`).
